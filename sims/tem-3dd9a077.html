The Structure of the Electron Gun

Thermionic Emission + Electric Field Acceleration
2600 K (Active)

Heats the cathode filament by an a.c. supply so electrons gain thermal energy to escape (thermionic emission).

200 kV

Produces a strong electric field between cathode (−) and cylindrical anode (+) to accelerate electrons to high speeds.

Step-by-Step Physics of the Electron Gun

  1. 1. Thermionic Emission at the Cathode (−) An alternating current (a.c.) passes through the tungsten filament (cathode), heating it to white-hot temperatures. Free electrons gain enough kinetic energy to overcome the work function of the metal.
  2. 2. High-Voltage Electric Field Acceleration A high d.c. accelerating voltage V (40 kV to 400 kV) is applied across the cathode (−) and the cylindrical anode (+). Electric potential energy is converted into kinetic energy:
    KE = eV = ½ mev²  ⇒  p = √(2meeV)
  3. 3. Why High Voltage Gives High Resolving Power By de Broglie's relation λ = h / p, faster electrons have a much shorter wavelength than visible light (~550 nm). According to the Rayleigh criterion (θmin ≈ 1.22 λ / d), a shorter wavelength produces much weaker diffraction and a much higher resolving power!

Why Magnetic Lenses Rotate the Image

A magnetic lens consists of copper coils enclosed in a soft-iron casing with N and S pole pieces.

Because the magnetic field inside the gap has both axial and radial components, an electron entering off-axis experiences a magnetic Lorentz force F = −e(v × B) that pushes it into a helical (spiral) path while converging toward the axis.
