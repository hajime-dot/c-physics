Why is Rayleigh Criterion Not Applicable?

Since diffraction is not involved in the formation of an STM image, the Rayleigh criterion does not apply. Resolving power is limited only by the size of the probe's tip (~0.01 nm lateral, ~0.001 nm vertical).

Table 3.2a: Direct Comparison of STM Scanning Methods

Watch both modes scan simultaneously across the exact same sample surface with a protruding central atom

Scanning with constant height

The probe scans horizontally across the sample surface at a constant height. The details of the surface are revealed by monitoring the change in the tunnelling current.

Advantage: Faster scan speed Risk: Tip may crash into tall bumps

Scanning with constant current

The probe scans across the sample surface with a constant current. The probe is moved up and down during scanning to keep the tip-to-surface distance unchanged. By monitoring the vertical movement of the probe, the details of the surface are revealed.

Advantage: Safe for rough surfaces Trade-off: Slower due to feedback loop
